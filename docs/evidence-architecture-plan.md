# Evidence architecture: retrieval, verification, articulation

Supersedes `docs/retrieval-plan.md` §2–3 (the Grok retry loop). That document's governing
rule survives unchanged and governs this one too:

> **A retry changes what we search for, never what counts as evidence.**

Compiled from five investigations: X's Community Notes scoring algorithm and
recommendation code, Perplexity's published architecture and API, OpenAI/Google/xAI
grounding surfaces, the academic fact-checking literature, and search/credibility
infrastructure pricing. Plus a measurement pass over our own pipeline, which turned out
to matter more than any of them.

---

## Part 1 — What is actually wrong, measured

Not inferred from the literature. Measured on our own code and providers on 2026-09-18.

### 1.1 We do not read sources. We read search snippets.

`WebSearchResolver.snapshot` says it plainly: *"No second fetch: the provider already
returned the page's content."* That content is a Tavily snippet, because `_search_tavily`
sends `search_depth: "basic"` and `include_raw_content: False`.

Across every reader-based verdict we have ever produced (`method =
'passage_entailment'`, n = 283):

| | chars |
|---|---|
| shortest quote | 63 |
| **median quote** | **209** |
| longest | 1,199 |

A 209-character quote is a fragment. `split_passages` needs 120 characters, so most
retrieved "sources" yield one or two passages with no surrounding text.

**Both misreads fixed on 2026-09-18 are symptoms of this, not causes.** A study
describing a myth read as *supporting* it; NOAA on the 2004 tsunami read as
*contradicting* a claim about Mumbai last week. Those are what a competent reader does
when handed a context-free fragment. The `asserted_by` and `contradiction_basis` fields
are worth keeping, but they were treating the symptom.

### 1.2 We search once, with the raw claim sentence — and we already pay for better

`candidates()` calls `self._search(claim.text)`. One query, the claim verbatim.

`search_phrases: list[str]` is generated by the extractor on **every single
extraction** (`app/extraction/llm.py:78`, and in the response schema we send the model)
and is never mapped onto `app/domain/claim.py`. Resolvers physically cannot see it. We
pay for query phrasings and discard 100% of them. `docs/retrieval-plan.md` §1 identified
this; it was never built.

### 1.3 The reader sees the top of the page, not the relevant part

`reasoning.py:445` — `selected = passages[:MAX_PASSAGES]`. The **first** 8.

Measured on a real article retrieved for "garlic prevents COVID":

```
[0] ![thepackerlogo.png](https://assets.farmjournal.com/…
[1] ![ Garlic](https://assets.farmjournal.com/…
[2] ***For more coronavirus coverage, check out our landing page…
[3] **Update**: California garlic nears with market riding high…
[4] [Snopes.com] said the web-fueled claim…      ← the evidence starts here
```

So **fixing 1.1 without fixing this would make us worse**: full text means more
boilerplate at the top, and the reader would see more of it. Perplexity reports the same
counter-intuitive result from their own span-labelling work — *smaller* claim-targeted
content budgets produced *more* accurate results than larger ones.

### 1.4 Retrieval quality is left to the search provider's defaults

- `search_depth: "basic"` returns worse *results*, not just less text. For one query,
  `basic` returned YouTube and Facebook; `advanced` returned NDTV, DW and SCMP.
- `include_domains` on Tavily is a **soft preference, not a filter** — probed with a
  nine-host health allowlist and got `thepacker.com` and `facebook.com` back. Client-side
  post-filtering is mandatory, not optional.
- Raw content brings its own noise: an `x.com` status page yielded 170 "passages" of
  navigation. The credibility gate and `is_navigation` matter *more* after this change,
  not less.

### 1.5 Articulation: rich data, canned prose, and two different things called "review"

The response already carries, per evidence item: tier, kind, direction, method, finding,
citation URL, verbatim excerpt, character anchors, match basis, match quality, publisher,
observation time — plus strength components each with their own citations. The data is
there.

What is missing is the sentence a person reads. `reason` is a fixed string per rule
("No evidence strong enough to decide this claim."), not an account of what the sources
said.

And we collapse two distinct outcomes into `SEND_TO_REVIEW`:

- **insufficient** — nothing citable was found
- **contested** — credible sources disagree with each other

Community Notes keeps these apart deliberately (`NEEDS_MORE_RATINGS` vs
`NEEDS_YOUR_HELP`). Telling a user "we couldn't verify this" when the truth is
"reputable sources disagree" is a different claim about the world.

Also: `VerifyRequest.mode` accepts `fast|deep` and **nothing in the codebase reads it**.
The contract hook for a deeper mode already exists.

## Part 2 — What the leaders do, and where the premise needs correcting

### 2.1 Their retrieval is genuinely better

| Mechanism | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Query fan-out**: one primary query restating the need, plus ~4 support queries exploring alternate phrasings and related entities | Perplexity's Q2D-Web paper (arXiv 2609.08887), built from ~23,000 production searches |
| **Later queries conditioned on what earlier ones returned** — iterative, not one-shot | same |
| ChatGPT does the same: "rewrites your query into one or more targeted queries", then issues *more specific* follow-ups after reading | OpenAI ChatGPT-search help article |
| **Hybrid lexical + dense retrieval, then cross-encoder rerank** | Perplexity architecture posts; BM25 alone 44.77 Recall@1000 vs 69.11 for their dense model |
| **Claim-conditioned span selection with a deliberately small budget** | Perplexity span-labelling post: smaller budgets produced *more* accurate results |
| **Three verbs, not a browser**: `search` → `open_page` → `find_in_page` | OpenAI deep-research; `find_in_page` is what keeps page tokens down |

### 2.2 But they make false claims constantly, and this corrects the brief

The premise "they never make false claims" does not survive contact with the
measurements. Independent, peer-reviewed and adversarial:

| System | Result |
|---|---|
| Perplexity | **49.0%** citation accuracy; **81.6%** overconfident (DeepTRACE, arXiv 2509.04499, 2,727 samples) |
| Perplexity | **37%** error rate attributing news — *best of eight tools tested* (Tow Center/CJR, 1,600 queries) |
| Perplexity Pro | cited **syndicated copies** of Texas Tribune articles in 3 of 10 queries — for its own publishing partner |
| ChatGPT Search | **67%** error rate; misidentified 134/200 articles, signalled low confidence 15 times, **never declined** |
| Grok-3 | **94%** error rate; **154 of 200 citations resolved to error pages** |
| Liu et al. (EMNLP Findings 2023) | only **51.5%** of generated sentences fully supported by their citations |
| FActScore (EMNLP 2023) | **36.0%** of supported and **37.6%** of unsupported sentences carried citations |

That last line is the one to internalise: **a citation marker is statistically unrelated
to whether the source supports the claim.** And industry-wide, refusals have fallen to
**0.5%** (EBU/BBC 2025, 2,709 responses across 14 languages) while significant-issue
rates stayed above 20%. They did not get more correct. They got more confident.

**Conclusion: copy their retrieval, keep our verdict discipline.** The evidence-tier
design law is not what holds us back — it is the only thing we have that they do not.
Nobody in the public record runs an entailment check between a cited source and the
sentence citing it. That is the differentiator, and it is cheap.

### 2.3 One thing we must not do

Gemini's *Grounding with Google Search* terms forbid caching Grounded Results,
"analyz[ing] … or otherwise learn[ing] from" them, and "using Links to build an index."
Our two core assets are a verdict cache and a host-credibility index built from observed
sources. That path is closed to us. Vertex's `checkGrounding` is a **separate product
with separate terms** — we supply the facts, Google only scores them — so the scorer
stays available even though the retrieval does not.

## Part 2.5 — The finding that reframes everything

Glockner, Hou & Gurevych, *"Missing Counter-Evidence Renders NLP Fact-Checking Unrealistic
for Misinformation"* (EMNLP 2022, arXiv 2210.13865), hand-analysed how professional
fact-checkers actually refuted 75 real false claims from PolitiFact and Snopes:

| How a human fact-checker refuted it | share |
|---|---|
| **Local counter-evidence** — tracing what the claimant relied on (the *source guarantee*) | **46.7%** |
| **Non-credible source** — the claim originated on a satire or hoax site | **18.7%** |
| Global counter-evidence — find a source that contradicts the claim | **26.7%** |
| **No-evidence assertion** — refuted *because* no trusted source supports it | **6.7%** |

**Our entire architecture implements the 26.7% case.** Roughly two thirds of real
fact-checking turns on *provenance* — where did this claim come from, and can that origin
be trusted — which we do not model at all.

This explains our two worst results directly. The fabricated Mumbai tsunami and the
fabricated Delhi earthquake got DO_NOT_SHARE by citing the 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami and
an unrelated magnitude-4.4 tremor, because "find contradicting evidence" is the only move
we have. The *correct* refutation is a **no-evidence assertion**: an event of that
magnitude would certainly be reported by every major outlet, we searched the sources that
would carry it, and none does. That is a legitimate, citable form of reasoning used by
real fact-checkers — and it is honest in a way that citing the wrong tsunami is not.

Two new capabilities follow, and they are the highest-value additions in this document:

**Provenance queries (stage 2b).** Alongside the claim's content, ask where the claim came
from: is this circulating from a known satire site, a hoax network, a partisan blog? The
AVeriTeC 2025 organisers include exactly this query type in their knowledge store and
nobody else implements it. Our credibility list is already the lookup table for it.

**Expected-coverage abstention (stage 8b).** For a claim whose truth would guarantee
coverage — a disaster, a death, a policy change, an election result — verified *absence*
across the sources that would certainly carry it is evidence. This needs two things we can
build: a judgement of whether the claim is coverage-guaranteed, and a record of which
Tier A/B sources were searched and returned nothing. Without the second, absence is just
ignorance; with it, absence is citable.

Note the honest limit: a no-evidence assertion is only sound when coverage really is
guaranteed and our source set really would carry it. For a local event in a district paper
we do not ingest, absence means nothing. The rule must require a *coverage-guaranteed*
claim class, not merely an empty result set.

## Part 3 — The architecture

Ten stages. Stages 1, 6 and 8 largely exist; the rest is the work.

```
1  decompose        claim → atomic checkable units          (exists)
2  plan queries     claim + search_phrases → ≤5 queries     (search_phrases exist, unplumbed)
3  retrieve         search API, full text, 10–20 results    (exists, wrong parameters)
4  gate sources     credibility label interface             (exists, needs an interface)
5  select passages  rank by relevance to THIS claim         (MISSING — the critical gap)
6  read stance      structured entailment per passage       (exists, hardened 2026-09-18)
7  weigh origins    decay within cohort, min across cohorts (exists as a set count)
8  decide           threshold + ordered veto stack          (exists as a rule ladder)
9  stress           leave-one-out ablation                  (MISSING)
10 articulate       contested ≠ insufficient, grounded prose (MISSING)
```

### Stage 2 — Query planning

Plumb `search_phrases` onto `Claim`. Then per claim issue **one primary query** (the
claim's checkable core) plus **up to four support queries** (alternate phrasings, the
named entity plus the predicate, the claim in the document's own language, and — for
health/science/policy claims — a site-scoped variant against the relevant credibility
category).

No new model call: the phrasings are already generated and paid for.

**Do not switch provider for this without reading the terms first.** Perplexity's Search
API is the cheapest fan-out on paper — up to 5 queries per request, billed once, ⇒ ~$1
per 1,000 queries at 50 QPS, median 358ms — but see §3.3a. Fan-out works on Tavily
today; it is a loop, not a provider feature.

### Stage 3 — Retrieve, with full text

`search_depth: "advanced"`, `include_raw_content: True`. Measured effect on one query:

| | snippet chars | raw chars | passages from raw |
|---|---|---|---|
| today | 140–1,502 | 0 | 0 |
| advanced + raw | 731–2,272 | **2,257–6,597** | **7–20** |

`include_raw_content` costs **zero extra credits**; only `advanced` depth costs 2 credits
instead of 1. The current settings are pure recall loss with no saving. The Exa path has
the same defect from the other direction — `maxCharacters: 1200` at
`app/sources/websearch.py:317`, when Exa bundles text and highlights free for the first
ten results.

This matters more than it looks, because **a citation requires locating a verbatim quote
in the returned text.** A 209-character snippet cannot support a quote that genuinely
exists on the page, so we were manufacturing our own abstentions.

Our own fetch is the fallback, not the primary route: on two probe URLs it gave
Wikipedia 9,841 chars / 29 passages and Newsweek **0 chars**. Record which route
produced the text (`extraction_route` already exists) so a snippet is never presented as
an article.

### 3.3a Provider choice is a licensing decision, not a price comparison

Most search vendors **forbid storing their results.** Our verdict cache, our evidence
table and our cache-version discipline all depend on storing them.

| Provider | Storing results |
|---|---|
| **Tavily** | **Permitted** — all 16 restrictions in §3.2 read; nothing about caching, storing, indexing or retaining output |
| Brave | Prohibited — *"store, cache, or create a database of Search Results … other than transient storage"*; the FAQ is blunter still |
| Exa | Prohibited (§4.2(a)), except browser cache; §1.2(c) also grants Exa a perpetual licence over our inputs **and outputs** |
| Google Programmable Search | Prohibited (§1.4(l)); also forbids reordering or filtering results — which is what our credibility gate does |
| Gemini Grounding with Search | Prohibited; results may only be shown to the user who submitted the prompt |
| Grounding with Bing | Prohibited; references must appear *"in the exact form provided by Microsoft"* |

**`websearch_provider` defaults to `tavily` (`app/config.py:201`) and that default is the
compliant one.** Written down here so nobody "optimises" to Brave or Exa later and
quietly breaks the cache's legal basis. Any provider change requires reading the storage
clause first.

One Tavily clause cuts our way and should be honoured deliberately — §6.6 forbids
*"automatic decision-making … relating to any person, which has a legal effect or a
similarly significant effect."* Our human-review queue is not only good epistemics, it is
a term of the licence we retrieve under.

Also dead or closing, so do not plan around them: **Bing Web Search API retired
2025-08-11**; **Google Custom Search JSON API is closed to new signups and shuts down
2027-01-01**; **Brave ended its free tier 2026-02-12**. SearxNG is not viable as a
production layer — it proxies engines that block it, and its own defaults suspend an
engine for **15 days** after a single CAPTCHA.

### 3.3b The fetch layer claims politeness it does not implement

Found while auditing, and all five are real:

1. **robots.txt is not implemented.** `app/security/fetcher.py:30` states *"Bulk ingestion
   identifies itself and respects robots"*. A grep for
   `robots|RobotFileParser|protego|can_fetch|crawl_delay` across `app/` returns **only
   that comment.** Add `protego` (wildcards, longest-match, `crawl_delay()`,
   `request_rate()`), and gate the feed and bulk paths on it.
2. **Conditional requests are dead code.** `feed.etag` and `feed.last_modified` exist in
   `migrations/0001_initial.sql:411-412`, and `app/knowledge/ingest.py:268` branches on
   `status_code == 304` — but `SecureFetcher.fetch()` takes no request headers, so
   `If-None-Match` and `If-Modified-Since` are never sent and that branch is
   unreachable. The politeness win we already designed has never run.
3. **The user agent points at a placeholder** — `FactifyBot/1.0
   (+https://factify.example/bot)`. A UA URL that 404s is worse for publisher relations
   than no URL at all.
4. **No per-host politeness**: no minimum interval, no `Retry-After` handling, no
   backoff, despite `fetcher.py:4` naming *"the politeness layer"*.
5. The text starvation in §3.3, which is the same neglect pointed at our own evidence.

When a publisher blocks us, the ladder degrades and never escalates: publisher feed or
sitemap → the snippet our licensed vendor already returned → an archival snapshot where
terms permit → **cite the URL with title and date, no quote, and let the evidence come
back insufficient.** That last rung is a feature: a citation requires a locatable quote,
so "we could not read this page" is an honest, auditable outcome. What we never do is
retry with a different proxy, user agent or fingerprint. Note that Firecrawl's
*recommended* default `proxy: "auto"` escalates to stealth mode precisely on 401/403/429
— if we ever use it, pin `proxy: "basic"`.

### Stage 4 — A credibility *label*, not a lookup in the rules

Today rules and resolvers consult `SourceCredibility` directly. Introduce one interface
that yields, per source: `credible`, `is_fact_checker`, `cohort` (ownership /
orientation), `tier_prior`. Veto rules read the label; they never query the host list.

This is the `UntrustedUrl` seam from X's code — one producer, many consumers — and it is
what lets us replace a hand-maintained allowlist with a learned or crowd judgement later
without touching the rule stack. With one correction to their design: **publish the
producer, not just the consumer.** Community Notes publishes raw ratings so anyone can
re-derive the scores; X publishes only the rules that consume `UntrustedUrl`, so nobody
can audit why a URL is untrusted. Ours must be auditable.

Note what an allowlist cannot do: it gives a per-host prior and says nothing about
whether *this particular report* is well-sourced. Community Notes has no allowlist at all
and judges sourcing per-note through a "diligence" model trained on
`notHelpfulSourcesMissingOrUnreliable` tags. We have no equivalent, and the allowlist
quietly hides that gap.

**Five layers, and only the first one gates.** `data/source_credibility.json` — 727 hosts,
35 categories — stays as the gate. Above it:

| Layer | Source | Role |
|---|---|---|
| 0 gate | our allowlist; weekly diff against `github.com/IFCN/verified-signatories` (**CC0**, 14 Indian domains), monthly EFCSN | admit / refuse |
| 1 tier | IFCN or EFCSN verified → Tier C; otherwise allowlisted → Tier D | already correct |
| 2 weight | Lin et al. ensemble, 11,520 domains, 0–1 (PNAS Nexus 2023 — six expert sets "correlated highly"); Tranco as a thin popularity prior | adjusts, never gates |
| 3 demote | Iffy Index (CC BY 4.0), WP:RSP `Deprecated`/`Blacklisted` | demotion only, never truth about a claim |
| 4 admit | the India ladder below | admits outlets no Western list covers |

Layer 2 will be **NULL for most Indian regional outlets**. That must mean "no adjustment",
never "excluded" — otherwise the weights quietly reimplement the Western-coverage bias
the list was built to avoid.

**The India legitimacy ladder** is the fix for that failure mode. For an outlet absent
from every Western list, accept any **two** of: PRGI/RNI registration (statutory, with a
publisher of record); **CBC/DAVP advertising empanelment — 7,000+ newspapers and
periodicals, the deepest Indian-language coverage of any list available**; INS, NBDA or
ABC India membership; appearing as a publisher in Google Fact Check Tools API results;
named in Media Ownership Monitor India; or a machine-checkable masthead test (named
editor, physical address, corrections policy, ownership disclosure). One offline scrape
into `data/india_registered.json` with a `provenance` field per host, refreshed monthly.

Our politician/party-ownership disqualifier stays the **only** India-specific exclusion.
Absence from a Western list is never grounds for exclusion.

**Two warnings.** Do not buy NewsGuard, MBFC or Ad Fontes as a gate: quote-only pricing,
redistribution forbidden, India coverage thin to absent — and MBFC now carries a published
academic repudiation (*"Stop using Media Bias/Fact Check in research"*, arXiv 2607.12108:
operated by one person, economic-system weighting is **35%** of the bias score, no
inter-rater reliability studies, and 42 surveyed papers mis-describe it as a fact-checking
organisation). And do not inherit MBFC *transitively* without noticing — **both the Iffy
Index and NELA-GT are MBFC-derived**, so its errors propagate into the free datasets we
would use at layers 2 and 3. Also avoid shipping NC-licensed data commercially: NELA-GT is
CC BY-NC, and Cloudflare Radar inside Tranco is CC BY-NC.

**PIB Fact Check needs an explicit policy.** Its own FAQ states it *"relies only on
official sources of information. News reports and other information published by private
organizations are not used."* So it is evidence of *what the government says about claims
concerning the government* — never an impartial adjudicator, and never Tier C. The legal
record supports treating it carefully: the 2023 IT Rules' binding government fact-check
unit was struck down by the Bombay High Court on 2024-09-26 (*Kunal Kamra v. Union of
India*) as violating Articles 14 and 19, with the Union's appeal pending and no stay
granted.

### Stage 5 — Passage selection (the critical missing stage)

Rank every passage of a fetched page against the claim, and send the reader the best few
rather than the first few:

1. score passages against the claim **two ways** — lexical (BM25 over the page's
   passages) and dense (the ONNX embedder we already load at startup),
2. **min-max normalise each score list, then take a convex combination.** Not a raw
   average, and preferably not RRF — see below,
3. drop navigation and boilerplate (`is_navigation` exists),
4. keep the top *k*, plus each kept passage's immediate neighbour for context,
5. cap the total character budget deliberately low.

This is the single change that converts stage 3 from a regression into an improvement,
and it costs nothing per call: both scorers are local.

**Why normalise, and why not just RRF.** Measured on BEIR-18 from Pyserini's own
published fusion runs (BM25-flat + bge-base-en-v1.5), nDCG@10:

| fusion | score |
|---|---|
| BM25 alone | 0.4244 |
| dense alone | 0.5083 |
| **raw score average** | **0.4400** — *worse than dense alone* |
| RRF, k=60 | 0.5153 |
| **min-max normalise, then average** | **0.5304** |

Normalisation alone is worth **9 points**; skipping it makes fusion actively harmful.
Bruch et al. (ACM TOIS, arXiv 2210.11934) reach the same ordering across 9 datasets at
p<0.01 — convex combination beats RRF everywhere — and report that *which* normaliser you
pick barely matters, only that you use one. **If we do use RRF, leave k at 60.** Tuning it
is a documented trap: RRF(10,4) lifts in-domain MS MARCO 0.425→0.451 but collapses
out-of-domain, HotpotQA 0.675→0.621.

**Why both scorers and not just the embedder.** Dense retrieval is not safe out of domain:
DPR scores **0.237** against BM25's **0.423** on BEIR-18, and even bge-base loses to BM25
by 18.5 points on one BEIR dataset (0.257 vs 0.442). Hybrid is a robustness mechanism, not
a scoring trick — Brewing BEIR found it beat BM25 on 16/18 datasets and the best single
model on 14/18, *"a level of robustness that none of the other models exhibit."*

**And the finding that applies most directly to us:** on BGE-M3's long-document benchmark,
**sparse beat dense by 8.8 nDCG@10 points** (57.5 vs 48.7), with fusion adding another
2.6–4.2. We are retrieving long web articles. Lexical scoring is not the legacy component
here.

**Two constraints on model choice.**

- **Licence.** Every Naver SPLADE checkpoint is **CC-BY-NC-SA-4.0** — unusable in a
  commercial product regardless of its numbers, and it costs 691 ms/query on one CPU core
  against BM25's 4 ms. `jinaai/jina-embeddings-v3` is CC-BY-NC-4.0, also out. The
  licence-clean options are MIT or Apache: `bge-small-en-v1.5` (33M, 384-d, MIT,
  MTEB-Retrieval 51.7 — the best quality-per-parameter point), `bge-base-en-v1.5` (109M,
  768-d, MIT, 53.3), `gte-base` (MIT), ColBERTv2 (MIT).
- **Multilinguality beats raw quality for us.** Those bge/gte models are **English-only**.
  We verify Hindi and eleven other Indian languages, so the multilingual embedder we
  already run stays the dense scorer. The English leaders are worth benchmarking only as
  an English-claim fast path, and `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` is disqualified anyway by its
  **256-token truncation**.

### Stage 5b — Score support with a small model, not the LLM

Every (claim, chunk) pair gets an entailment score from a dedicated model. This is the
cheapest reliable signal in the whole pipeline and it should carry the verdict; the LLM
should arbitrate, not adjudicate.

| Option | Params | Licence | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`vectara/hallucination_evaluation_model`** (HHEM-2.1-Open) | 0.1B | **Apache-2.0** | **<600 MB RAM, ~1.5s per 2k tokens on x86 CPU**, unlimited context, returns a probability. BAcc 76.55 |
| `yaxili96/FactCG-DeBERTa-v3-Large` | 0.4B | **MIT** | LLM-AggreFact **75.6** — above MiniCheck at half the size |
| `lytang/MiniCheck-Flan-T5-Large` | 770M | MIT | 74.7, and a **644 MB Q6_K GGUF** exists for llama.cpp |

MiniCheck's own accounting: **$0.24 versus GPT-4's $107** to score 13,000 examples —
446× cheaper for 0.6 BAcc less. Avoid SummaC (its 24 shared ALBERT layers cost ~1.2B of
compute and it runs an M×N sentence matrix), anything CC-BY-NC (Bespoke-MiniCheck-7B,
Lynx, GLIDER), and every 11B NLI model (TRUE scores **61.0** at 30× the cost).

**Pair at chunk × claim-sentence granularity, never document level.** SummaC's ablation is
unambiguous: full document × full claim scores **56.4**, sentence × sentence **74.4**.

### Stage 6 — Stance reading

Unchanged from the 2026-09-18 hardening: the model names who asserts the statement
(`asserted_by`) and what a contradiction rests on (`contradiction_basis`), and code
enforces the consequence. Two additions to evaluate, both cheap:

- **MiniCheck** (arXiv 2404.10774) — 770M parameters, sentence-level "is this claim
  grounded in this document", reported at GPT-4 accuracy for ~1/400th the cost, runs
  locally on CPU alongside our existing ONNX models. No per-call cost, no terms of
  service. Use as a second opinion on decisive evidence.
- **Vertex `checkGrounding`** — the only API in the industry returning *per-claim support
  scores*: `supportScore` overall plus per-claim `score ∈ [0,1]`, span offsets, and the
  supporting chunk text, at **$0.00075 per 1,000 calls**, sub-500ms, 200 facts per call.
  It is a pure scorer, indifferent to who generated the text or retrieved the facts.
  **Offsets are byte-based, not character-based** — a silent trap for Devanagari.

### Stage 7 — Weighing origins

Replace the set-of-registrable-domains count with two mechanisms borrowed from X:

**Graded decay within a cohort.** The Nth report from one ownership cluster contributes

```
(1 - floor) * decay**index + floor        # decay 0.5, floor 0.25 in X's home-mixer
```

so genuine reporting depth still earns more than a single article, converging to a floor
instead of scaling linearly. Strictly more expressive than "at most one per publisher".

**`min` across cohorts, not a sum.** Community Notes' explicit bridging rule is

```
netMinHelpful = max(0, min(negFactor_net, posFactor_net))
```

— the strength of the evidence is the strength of its **weakest** cohort. Ported:
partition supporting and contradicting evidence by ownership/orientation cohort, and let
the smaller side bound the conclusion.

**Same-side dissent counts for more.** Their two best filters both weight criticism
highest when it comes from raters *closest* to the note's own viewpoint
(`weight = 1/(1+(d/d₄₀)**5)`). Ported: a source that normally aligns with a claim's
direction but contradicts it outweighs an opposed source saying the same thing. A
pro-government outlet reporting a government claim false is the strongest evidence
available.

**Discount by base rate.** A publisher that debunks everything carries less weight per
debunk: divide by the log of that host's own contradiction propensity, computed from our
verdict history (`tf_idf_incorrect` in their `incorrect_filter.py`).

**Freeze the priors.** Publisher cohort and reliability are estimated offline over a long
window and held fixed while a claim is scored — `freezeRaterParameters=True` in their
final round. Never let one claim's evidence move a publisher's prior in the same pass;
that is how feedback loops start.

### Honest limits of the bridging analogy

Recorded here so nobody re-derives them later:

- **We cannot fit the viewpoint axis.** Their matrix factorisation needs ~1M raters
  co-rating a shared pool. We have 3–15 sources per claim, most appearing once. The axis
  must be supplied *exogenously* from ownership data — which inverts their central
  principle of deliberately not assuming who is on which side. Our version carries an
  editorial judgement theirs does not.
- **Our adversary is syndication, not brigading.** Ten outlets running one wire story.
  Resolve wire pickups to their origin before counting; today's excerpt-identity merge is
  a start, not a solution.
- **Publishers assert; raters rate.** A rater emits a scalar on a shared item. A
  publisher emits a document we must first interpret into a stance. That interpretation
  is where our error budget actually goes, and it has no analogue in their pipeline.
- **A `min`-over-cohorts rule returns "unsettled" whenever one side has no qualifying
  publisher** — which happens often when coverage is skewed, and not because the claim is
  false. Whether that means unsettled or means the covered side wins is a policy
  decision, and it must be made deliberately.
- **They started with the elegant implicit criterion and spent two years bolting explicit
  quorum checks onto it.** Every change since 2025-03 adds more hard both-sides gating.
  Skip that journey: make the rule explicit from the start.

### Stage 7b — The sufficiency gate runs *before* the verdict model

This is the single most important structural rule in the plan, and the literature is
unanimous on why. In the 2025 AVeriTeC shared task **no system exceeded 0.10** on
"not enough evidence" or "conflicting evidence"; several teams dropped the labels
entirely because guessing "Refuted" pays when Refuted is 71.9% of the data. Measured on
human-labelled claims: **64.7% of genuinely conflicting claims were confidently called
Refuted, and 40.5% of genuinely-insufficient claims got a hard verdict.**

An abstention path that is a branch *inside* the verdict prompt does not fire. It has to
be **a gate ahead of the verdict model, deciding on signals that model never sees**:

- highest entailment score below threshold → **INSUFFICIENT**, stop.
- support and contradiction both present above threshold → **CONTESTED**, stop.
- otherwise proceed to the verdict call.

Published starting points to calibrate against, not adopt blindly: EFC gates on retrieval
similarity (`cos[0] < 0.82` → insufficient; `Var(cos) > 0.0007` → conflicting) with **zero
extra model calls**, and was the fastest system in the field at 7.01 s/claim at
baseline-parity score. R2VC abstains below verifier score **0.55** or calibrated
confidence **0.60**.

**And more retrieval is not automatically better.** RAGuard (NeurIPS 2025) found *every*
model tested performed worse with retrieval than without on a binary fact-checking task,
and misleading-retrieval recall climbing from **21.3% at one document to 44.8% at five**.
Corrective RAG made it worse still. Spend the budget on query fan-out and evidence
*diversity* (MMR), then prune hard with stage 5b — not on retrieving more and hoping the
model sorts it out.

### Stage 8 — Decide: a cheap bar plus an ordered veto stack

Community Notes' actual acceptance test is one line — `numRatings >= 5 &&
intercept >= 0.40` — and everything trustworthy about it lives in ~10 veto rules applied
afterwards, each a pure function with declared dependencies, run in order, **last rule
wins**, and the deciding rule recorded as `decidedBy`.

Our rule ladder is already close. What to adopt: name each veto, make each independently
ablatable, and record which one decided — `decided_by` is free explainability and we need
it for articulation anyway. Their internal `FIRM_REJECT` status is also worth copying: a
verdict that is never shown but blocks weaker downstream scorers from promoting.

Add hysteresis: `inertiaDelta = 0.01` — a verdict must fall *below* the bar to be
revoked, not merely fail to clear it, so verdicts do not oscillate as web results churn.
**This conflicts with our cache-version discipline** (bumping `RULE_VERSION` retires
cached verdicts by design). An explicit policy is needed for which wins.

### Stage 9 — Stress the verdict before publishing it

Recompute the decision twice: once without the single highest-weight source, once without
the largest ownership cohort. **If either flips the verdict, it is not settled.**

This is their leave-one-out ablation (they re-run the whole pipeline without the top 0.1%
of raters and without correlated raters, requiring the ablated score to clear 0.37 where
the normal bar is 0.40). It costs us nothing extra: stance per document is already
computed and cached, so the ablation is arithmetic over existing evidence, not new calls.

### Stage 10 — Articulate the verdict

Three changes, in order of value:

1. **Split `SEND_TO_REVIEW`** into `CONTESTED` (credible sources disagree) and
   `INSUFFICIENT` (nothing citable found). Different sentences, different user actions.
2. **Ground the reason in the sources.** Compose the explanation from the evidence we
   already hold — publisher, tier, direction, the verbatim quote, the date — rather than
   a fixed string per rule. Every sentence must map to an evidence id; nothing in the
   prose may exist without one. Then verify the composed paragraph against its own
   citations with stage 6's machinery, so the explanation is held to the same standard as
   the verdict.
3. **Report consulted *and* cited.** Every major provider converged on this split:
   inline citations for display, a full consulted set for audit. "We read 14 sources; 9
   were tier A or B; 3 carried this claim" is both better disclosure and a quality
   signal we can track.

Never let the model emit a URL. The citation comes from the fetch record, and the
extraction schema has no field a link could be written into. That is not an oversight;
it is the guarantee. CJR found Grok-3 returning 154 dead links out of 200, and
Perplexity's own docs warn that asking for links inside structured output produces
fabricated ones.

**Verify the explanation with the same machinery as the verdict.** After composing the
prose, re-run stage 5b on each (justification sentence, cited chunk) pair and drop or
rewrite any sentence that does not clear the bar. This closes a loop the whole field
leaves open: Xing et al. (NAACL 2025) measured that **only 31% of GPT-4 explanations are
fully attributed even with human-curated evidence, and 9% with retrieved evidence.** Their
conclusion is the one to act on — *explanation quality is capped by evidence selection, not
by the generator.* Evaluate on REFLEX's axes (misleadingness, informativeness, soundness,
readability), never ROUGE against a reference.

And what working fact-checkers actually asked for (CHI 2025, interviews): **trace the
reasoning path, reference specific evidence, surface the gaps.** Worth noting that Full
Fact — the most mature deployed operation in this space — **does not issue automated
verdicts at all**; its automation does claim detection and matching against prior
fact-checks, and leaves adjudication to journalists. Our review queue is not a weakness
relative to the state of the art; it is the state of the art.

### A concrete bug this research found in our code

Our reasoning schema lists `relation` second and `reason` sixth, so the model **emits its
verdict before its reasoning.** Structured-output models fill fields in schema order, and
Tam et al. (EMNLP 2024 Industry) traced GPT-3.5's GSM8K collapse from **75.99 to 49.25**
to exactly this — *"100% of JSON-mode responses placed the 'answer' key before the
'reason' key,"* turning chain-of-thought into direct answering. Reorder every response
schema so reasoning precedes the decision. Also prefer grammar-constrained decoding to
naive JSON mode; constrained decoding does **not** show the same degradation.

## Part 4 — What we refuse, and why

| Refused | Reason |
|---|---|
| Gemini Grounding with Google Search as a retrieval backend | Its terms forbid caching results and building an index from returned links. Our product is a verdict cache plus a host index. |
| Model-generated citations | The schema has no URL field. See Grok's 154/200 dead links. |
| Bot-detection evasion, CAPTCHA solving, fingerprint spoofing | Not what makes these systems good — their advantage is query planning, extraction and attribution. Also: `Perplexity-User` and `ChatGPT-User` *do* bypass robots.txt for user-initiated fetches, and CJR caught both reading content they should not have. That is a liability to avoid, not a pattern to copy. |
| A model deciding what counts as evidence | Generating a search phrase is not deciding a verdict. The line stays where `retrieval-plan.md` drew it. |
| Deep research on every claim | Perplexity's own worked example: 21 queries, 27 citations, **$0.816 for one request** — roughly 1,000× a cheap grounded call. |
| A search provider whose terms forbid storing results | The verdict cache is the product. See §3.3a. |
| NewsGuard / MBFC / Ad Fontes as a gate | Quote-only pricing, redistribution forbidden, India coverage absent, and MBFC is academically repudiated. |
| PIB Fact Check as an adjudicator | By its own policy it consults only official sources. Evidence of the government's position, not a verdict. |

**Budget reality.** At ~1,000 verifications/day, **search dominates cost, not the model** —
roughly 200× the per-claim cost of reasoning. Tavily's free 1,000 credits/month is ~33
searches/day; Bootstrap at $100/month is ~500/day. Fan-out multiplies this by the number
of queries per claim, so the step budget in stage 2 is a cost control, not a nicety.

## Part 5 — Sequence

Ordered by value per hour, with the two-day hackathon deadline in mind.

**Before the deadline — four changes, each small and measurable**

1. Stage 3: two parameters (`search_depth: "advanced"`, `include_raw_content: True`).
2. Stage 5: passage selection by claim relevance, normalised hybrid score. **Must land
   with 3, not after it** — 3 alone is a regression, because the reader currently sees the
   first 8 passages and full text means more boilerplate at the top.
3. **Reorder the response schemas so reasoning precedes the decision.** A few lines, no
   new calls, and the measured downside of getting it wrong is large.
4. Stage 10.1: split `CONTESTED` from `INSUFFICIENT`.

Stage 2's query fan-out is *nearly* free (the phrasings are already generated) but it
multiplies search spend per claim and RAGuard warns that more documents can hurt. Land it
with the sufficiency gate, not before.

Acceptance is the existing rule, unchanged from `retrieval-plan.md`: **abstention falls
and the false-positive rate does not move.** If abstention falls and false positives
rise, the change lowered the bar and must be reverted. The 46-claim matrix from
2026-09-18 is the baseline — zero false positives, zero false negatives, 13/18 true
claims cleared, 14/20 false claims blocked.

**After the deadline — the verdict layer, highest value first**

5. **Stage 2b + 8b: provenance queries and expected-coverage abstention.** Per Part 2.5
   this is two thirds of real fact-checking and we implement none of it. It is also what
   replaces our two worst outputs (fabricated disasters refuted by unrelated disasters)
   with an honest citation.
6. Stage 7b: the sufficiency gate ahead of the verdict model.
7. Stage 5b: HHEM-2.1-Open on CPU for per-chunk support scoring.
8. Stage 7: cohort decay, `min` across cohorts, same-side dissent, base-rate discount.
9. Stage 9: the ablation gate.
10. Stage 10.2–10.3: grounded prose verified against its own citations, consulted-vs-cited
    reporting.
11. Stage 4: the label interface; the India registration ladder.

**Evaluation.** Build the harness on AVeriTeC's four labels and QA-pair evidence format
(⚠️ the dataset is **CC BY-NC 4.0** — internal evaluation only, never shipped), and score
with **Ev2R recall at t = 0.5** using our own cheap model as judge. Never Hungarian METEOR:
it correlates with human judgement at **ρ = −0.029**, against Ev2R's **0.500**. Track
**per-class** scores, because the field average is Supported .22 / Refuted .28 /
**insufficient .03 / conflicting .02** — an aggregate number hides exactly the failure we
care about. Always report against the **majority-class floor of 61–72%**.

**Two cautions on decomposition, which we already do.** It is not a free win: Hu et al.
measured decomposition *costing* up to **17 F1 points** with a strong verifier, and
MiniCheck reports "near-zero change for GPT-4". It pays only when each sub-claim gets its
**own retrieval pass** (Akhter et al.: repeated claim-level evidence was not significant at
p=0.124; sub-claim-aligned evidence was, at p<0.0001). And decomposers hallucinate at very
different rates — VeriScore 0.8%, SAFE 3.4%, DnD 10.9% — with the most prolific being the
worst. Our anchor requirement (a claim must be locatable in the source text) is a stronger
guard than any of those systems has.

**Deliberately not scheduled**

- Our own crawler and index. Every mechanism above is reachable through per-request
  APIs; Perplexity's 200-billion-URL index is not the part we are missing.
- A learned reranker. The local embedder plus a small cross-encoder covers stage 5 until
  volume justifies more.

## Part 6 — Decisions needed before building

1. **When one side has no coverage**, does `min`-across-cohorts mean *unsettled*, or does
   the covered side win? This determines the abstention rate more than any threshold.
2. **Hysteresis vs cache-version discipline.** Locking a verdict and retiring it on a
   rule bump are contradictory. Which wins?
3. **Search provider.** Tavily today (1,000/month free); Perplexity's Search API is
   ~$1/1k queries with 5-query fan-out billed as one request. Benchmark, then choose.
4. **Where to sit on the groundedness/usefulness curve.** Liu et al. measured citation
   precision at **r = −0.96** against perceived utility: answers closest to their sources
   read as least useful. This is a product decision, not a technical one.
